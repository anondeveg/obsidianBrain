# FOO Bar Foobar

1. **“Foo,” “Bar,” and “Foobar” are placeholders**, not real words — they’re _metasyntactic variables_ used in examples when the actual name doesn’t matter.
    
2. **The term “Foo” predates computing**, appearing in 1930s comic strips like _Smokey Stover_, where “FOO” was a nonsense word meaning roughly “nonsense itself.”
    
3. **“Foobar” likely came from “FUBAR,”** a WWII military slang acronym meaning " Fucked Up Beyond All Recognition/Repair.”
    
4. **Tech culture adopted these words early,** first in MIT and ARPANET circles, then in UNIX documentation, then in IETF RFCs — by 2001, about 7% of all RFCs had used them.
