## Cephalus’ Definition
**Morality is, without qualification, truthfulness and giving back anything one has borrowed from someone.**

### Socrates’ Refutation
Returning what one owes is not always moral. Example: returning weapons to a friend who has become insane would be wrong. Therefore, the definition is incomplete.

---

## Polemarchus’ Definition (from Simonides)
**Morality is giving each person what one owes them**, clarified as giving what is appropriate. This becomes: **doing good to one’s friends and harm to one’s enemies.**

### Socrates’ Refutation
Morality is a human virtue that cannot be used to make someone worse. Harming people makes them worse. Therefore, it is never right to harm anyone, so the definition collapses.

---

## Thrasymachus’ Definition (Advantage of the Stronger)
**Morality is nothing other than the advantage of the stronger party.**  
Rulers make laws for their own benefit; therefore subjects act morally when they act to the rulers’ advantage.

### Socrates’ Refutation
Rulers sometimes make mistakes. If subjects obey harmful laws, morality becomes disadvantageous to the stronger, contradicting the definition.

Using the analogy of crafts, Socrates argues that **every craft benefits its subject, not its practitioner**. Thus ruling, as a craft, benefits the ruled, not the ruler. So morality is not simply the advantage of the stronger.

---

## Thrasymachus’ Second Claim (Superiority of Immorality)
**Immorality = sound judgement or cleverness.**  
**Morality = simplicity.**  
Immorality, especially on a grand scale, is more profitable, powerful, and self-advantageous.

### Socrates’ Refutation
Moral people are good and clever; immoral people are ignorant and bad, seeking advantage over both good and bad alike. Immorality resembles incompetence.

Using the functional argument:  
- Every function has a virtue.  
- The mind’s function is living, managing, ruling.  
- Morality is the mind’s virtue.  
- Morality enables the mind to perform its function well; immorality makes it perform badly.  
Therefore, immorality is never more rewarding than morality.
